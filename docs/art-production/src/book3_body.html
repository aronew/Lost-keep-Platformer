How to use this book

Start with the pilot: W1, W3 and C1.

W1 decides the level's stone, W3 is its most recognisable set piece, and C1 is the first enemy that fights. Make the rest once those three read well in 1-1.

  1. Attach the images each card lists. The platformer's references are in docs/art-production/platformer-attach/; the lettered ones (A, B, Z) are Book 1's, in docs/art-production/lostkeep-attach/. The mockup M1 goes with every card.
  2. Press Copy and paste the prompt into your image model. The copy includes the shared rules unless you untick the box at the top.
  3. Check the result against the checklist. If it falls short, use a fix from If the image falls short.
  4. Save it under the card's file name and hand it to me exactly as the generator made it. Don't run it through another cleanup or export tool. I reduce it to true pixel scale and place it in 1-1, and we judge it in the game.
  5. Tick it done. Ticks are saved in this browser only.

How a level is assembled

The same method as Lost Keep's rooms, with a platformer's demands on top. These rules are why the cards ask for what they ask for.

Black box, then skin

  • Every level is blocked out as black masses (#0B0812) with flat floors: that is the collision, approved before any art.
  • Terrain art is painted along the edges of those masses and steps down into the black inside them, like a shadow. Only walking lines are straight.
  • Set pieces (the stall, the islands, the log) are cut to the collision's exact shape: each card gives the walking line and the openings in pixels.

Natural, like Donkey Kong Country

  • Everything Kleo stands on is something real: ground, a stone terrace, a fallen log, a branch, a treetop. No floating planks.
  • Things that act (a chest, a crate, a cracked wall) look like they were left there by people who lived here.
  • Secrets have a reason to be there and a small visible tell.

Parallax and light

  • Sky 0.05 · far 0.15 · aqueduct 0.25 · mid forest 0.35 · playfield 1.0 · one black plant band in front.
  • Lanterns are lights in the engine: paint the flame and the lit glass, never the halo.
  • Each backdrop is hazier and bluer than the one in front of it.

Sprites

  • Creatures and Kleo are animated sheets: every frame on its own cell, feet on one shared line.
  • Animation must feel great: anticipation, a strong key pose, follow-through. The Thornhound's wind-up is the player's warning, so it has to read.

Check every result

Every image

  • Put it next to M1. It looks like the same artist made both.
  • It is real pixel art: hard square pixels on one even grid, no blur, no soft gradients.
  • No text, labels, grid lines or watermarks; transparent where the card says so.
  • No red anywhere: no red caps, roots, banners or fire. Crimson belongs to the Blight alone.

Stone and set pieces

  • Every walking line is dead straight, at exactly the height the card gives.
  • Openings and widths match the card's numbers: lay a ruler on them.
  • Masses step into the black along the stones' own outlines, never along a straight line.
  • Undersides and ceilings break up with hanging moss, roots and wedges.

Sprites

  • Every frame sits on its own cell, feet on the same line.
  • Shrunk to game size, each pose reads as a clear silhouette.
  • The Thornhound's wind-up is obviously a warning.

Backdrops

  • The left and right edges join.
  • It is hazier and lower in contrast than the forest in front of it.
  • Nothing in it looks like a ledge she could stand on.

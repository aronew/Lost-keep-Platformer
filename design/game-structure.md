# Game structure (v0.2, approved by the creative lead)

> Approved as written ("Approved, start on the systems then the 1-1
> blockout"), with their additions from Donkey Kong Country folded in (§5:
> the land, things to carry and throw, cracked walls and bonus games, secret
> routes high above). Choices marked **Proposed** were mine; they stand
> unless the creative lead overrules them.

## 1. The shape of the game

- **Seven regions**, each a biome, played in order on a world map of
  Vaelora. Each is a run of levels ending in a **boss level**.
- **Outdoors first.** The first four regions are open air and colourful; the
  game goes under roofs and underground only in the last three, when the
  story goes into the Vexari's works.
- **Each boss gives Kleo a new ability**, and the next region is built around
  it (as Rayman Origins does world by world). The ability changes how she
  plays from then on; it does **not** open locks in old levels.
- **Not a Metroidvania:** no interconnected map, no backtracking, no
  ability-gated secrets. **Every secret in a level can be reached with the
  abilities Kleo has when she first plays it.** Replaying a level is for
  finding what you missed, not for using a new key.
- **Between regions, the Refuge** (the hub): rescued people live there, and
  Resin is spent there.
- **Length (Proposed):** about 5 to 7 minutes per level on a first play,
  about 45 to 60 minutes per region, **6 to 8 hours** for the story and about
  10 to 12 with every secret.

## 2. Levels per region: 7 (5 levels, 1 secret level, 1 boss)

**Proposed: 5 main levels + 1 hidden secret level + 1 boss level = 7 stops
per region, 49 in all** (instead of 10 per region, 70 in all).

Why not 10:
- **Each level should own one idea** (teach it, test it, twist it, §5). A
  region has about five strong ideas: its new ability, its signature machine,
  its signature hazard, its enemy mix, and the vertical climb. Ten levels
  would mean five levels repeating an idea, which is filler.
- **The art kit is per region.** Ten levels on one kit start to look the same;
  five carry a kit without repeating, and each region still gets a full look.
- **Benchmarks:** Donkey Kong Country runs about 6 levels a world (40 in
  all), DKC 2 about 6, DKC Returns 6 to 9 including bonus levels, Rayman
  Origins 6 to 10. Five plus a secret plus a boss sits right in that range.
- **AAA presentation per level** is the priority. 49 handcrafted levels is a
  big game already; 70 at that bar is roughly a third more work for no new
  ideas.

Within each region:

| Stop | Role |
|---|---|
| X-1 | **Arrival.** The region's look and its new ability, taught gently. Short. |
| X-2 | **The signature machine**: the region's own moving thing (a raft, a lift, a rolling log), used sparingly. |
| X-3 | **The vertical level**: a climb, about 2 screens wide and 4 to 5 high. |
| X-4 | **The signature hazard**, with the region's enemies at full strength. |
| X-5 | **The gauntlet:** everything the region taught, mixed and twisted. Hides the secret exit. |
| X-S | **Secret level** (found through a hidden exit, usually in X-5 or X-4). Harder, short, a big reward. |
| X-B | **Boss level:** a short approach, then the arena. The ability is the reward. |

The vertical level can move (X-2 or X-4) where a region's story wants it.

## 3. The regions, in order

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | **The Hearth-Plains** | Golden fields at late afternoon, silk orchards, windmills, Rootling villages, hay barns, butterflies | The Ward (parry, bash-dash), bounce silk, windmill sails, hay carts | **The Silk Mother** | **Barkgrip** |

| 5 | **The Carapax Foundry** | Vexari war-works on a volcanic shelf: smokestacks and gantries outside, forges inside; brass, soot, molten gold | Wing-Cloak (glide) over molten channels, heat-vent updrafts, conveyors, crushers, pistons | **The Carapax Prototype** | **Thorn-Line** |

| 7 | **The Anima Vats** | The harvesting works under the Spire: vats of teal Anima, Blight crystal growths, iron, icy-blue Prism light at the very end | Anima Siphon (drain Blight crystals, power machines); everything at once | **The Alchemist** | The finale: the Sovereign Prism |

### Why this order
- **Colour and mood run from lively to grave, bright to dark.** The
  creative lead: "we can go from a brighter setting to a darker setting in
  later regions". Region 1 is bright day; then gold fields, violet dusk,
  green swamp in the open air; then fire, sky, and finally the vats. Darker
  regions stay readable: every hazard and creature is seen at a glance. You
  asked for colourful outdoors first; the first underground level comes
  about four hours in.
- **Story:** Kleo leaves home (1), follows the trail of the missing into the
  farmlands where the Silk Mother is cocooning villagers (2), crosses the
  wastes to win the Ash-Walkers' fire (3), finds the Blight's source growing
  in Thornveil (4), breaks into the war-works that make it (5), climbs to the
  Spire where the High Voice is deceived, and frees Vesper: **the Reveal**
  (6), then goes down into the vats to end it (7).
- **Each ability is born where the story puts it:** the Ward is her captain's
  shield, taken from the fallen warden; Barkgrip is made by the Ironbark smith
  freed from the Silk Mother's great cocoon; Emberwick is the Ash-Walkers'
  fire-craft; the Reaper's stolen Anima flows back into Kleo and wakes her
  dormant wings (Wing-Cloak); Morvath, whose thorn knots have been watching
  since region 1, meets Kleo at the Foundry's broken gate and gives her the
  Thorn-Line; the Siphon is Vexari technology taken from a Shepherd.
- **The seven planned bosses fit the seven regions exactly.** The Lost Keep
  regions with no boss (the Deep-Glow Caverns, the Tide-Weaver coast,
  Morvath's Observatory) are kept for later: a bonus region, DLC, or a
  sequel. Sora (Deep-Glow) is rescued in Thornveil instead.

### The difficulty curve
Each region's platforming is harder than the last. Hard numbers keep it
honest (Kleo's envelope is in `CLAUDE.md`):

| Region | Widest required gap | Smallest landing | Timing | Enemies | Checkpoints |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 tiles (a standing jump does it) | 3 tiles | Generous, nothing chases | One at a time | 1, at the middle |
| 2 | 5 tiles | 2 tiles | Platforms move slowly, in sight | Pairs | 1, at the middle |
| 3 | 6 tiles (a running jump) | 2 tiles | Timed hazards (geysers) | Ranged enemies added | 1, at the middle |
| 4 | Running jump into a wall jump | 2 tiles, sinking | Water rises on a clock | Mixed groups | 1, at the middle |
| 5 | Glides of 12+ tiles | 1 tile | Machines in sequence | Armoured Carapax | 1, at the middle |
| 6 | Grapple chains over open sky | Knots only | Crumbling spires, wind | Flyers | 1, at the middle |
| 7 | Everything chained, no rest between | 1 tile | Tight | Everything | 1, at the middle |

Later regions also combine: a region's twist is often an earlier region's
idea meeting the new ability (a glide through region 3's ember geysers in the
Foundry; a wall jump up a crumbling High-Spire tower).

## 4. Abilities

Kleo starts with Lost Keep's base kit: **run, jump, ledge catch, the 4-hit
sword combo** (swipe left, swipe right, overhead, a spinning leap into a
slashing slam; she steps forward with every hit), and her **Lantern** (it
lights dark corners and pulses near hidden letters and bonus entrances).

**Changed (the creative lead, 2026-11): power-ups, as Mario's, not
permanent abilities.** "A platformer side scroller [with] the fun fighting
you'd get from an action adventure", puzzle sections, and "like in Mario
where you get power-ups, a shooter, a star ... but in this game you get
magic, ward". The rules:
- A power-up is **found inside a level**, she **holds one at a time**, and
  **a hit costs the power instead of a flame** (it is an extra hit). Taking
  another replaces it. A defeat loses it. It returns where it hung when she
  is sent back to a checkpoint.
- **The sword is the constant**; a power-up adds to it.
- **Beating a boss unlocks a new kind**, which then turns up in levels (and
  in earlier levels on a replay). Levels never need a power to finish; a
  power opens shortcuts, secrets and easier fights.

| Power-up | Unlocked | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Anima Spark** (built) | From the start | The spell button looses a teal bolt, level, about 11 tiles: strikes what a sword strikes |
| **Ironbark Ward** | Region 1's boss | Proposed: a shield that turns shots back and lets her bash through bark-plate |
| **Emberwick** | Region 2's boss | Proposed: a burning blade: burns bramble and webs, lights braziers (puzzles) |
| **Morvath's Star** | Region 3's boss | Proposed: a short rush, unhurt, felling what she touches |
| **Wing-Cloak** | Region 4's boss | Proposed: hold jump to glide (Mario's cape) |

The table below is the earlier plan (a permanent ability after each boss),
kept as the source of these powers.

| After | Ability | What it does in a platformer |
|---|---|---|
| Region 1 | **Ironbark Ward** | Parry (about 8 frames: a parried shot flies back) and a 3-tile ground bash-dash that breaks bark-plate and hay and slips under closing gates |
| Region 2 | **Barkgrip** | Cling to wood and root walls (slides slowly on stone), wall jump about 2.5 tiles up and away |
| Region 3 | **Emberwick** | A short flame cone from the Lantern: burns bramble, silk and webs, lights braziers (which open doors and, later, make updrafts) |
| Region 4 | **Wing-Cloak** | Hold jump to glide (about 3 across per 1 down); rides updrafts |
| Region 5 | **Thorn-Line** | Fire at a thorn knot within 7 tiles and swing or pull to it |
| Region 6 | **Anima Siphon** | Drain Blight crystals (clearing Blight mist and Blightwater) and give Anima to dead machines |

**The Anima spells** (Spark, Lance) and **Memory-Meld** are left out of the
first plan to keep the kit lean; Memory-Meld survives as the story's Reveal.
They can come back as late additions if the kit feels thin.

**Her stride** (built; the creative lead, on playing it: "the boost is cool"; from the
research: Donkey Kong's roll, Mario's run). Run on for a second without
stopping and Kleo finds her stride: about a third faster (182 px/s against
140), with a jump of 8.3 tiles against 6.4. It lasts through jumps while she
keeps moving; stopping, turning, a hit or a swing loses it. It adds length,
never height, so no secret is reached by it alone; it is speed as the reward
for flow, and the way a skilled player skips a stone. Levels are still
designed for the plain jump: every required gap can be made without it.

## 5. How a level is laid out

### Size
- **Horizontal levels:** about **12 to 16 screens long** (480 to 640 tiles,
  7,700 to 10,000 px) and **1 to 2 screens tall**, with dips and rises. At
  her run speed that's about a minute of pure running; with the fighting,
  jumping and looking around, **5 to 7 minutes**. Region 1's levels are
  shorter (10 to 12 screens).
- **Vertical levels:** about **2 screens wide and 4 to 5 high** (80 tiles by
  90 to 112). The camera rises to a new floor when she lands on it, never on
  a jump (the camera rules stand).

### Four beats: teach, test, twist, finish
Every level has **one idea** (a machine, a hazard, an enemy, or the new
ability) and plays it in four beats:

1. **Teach** (about 1 screen in, no danger of falling): show the idea on its
   own where failing costs nothing. A moving platform first moves over solid
   ground before it ever crosses a pit.
2. **Test**: the idea with a real cost: a pit, water, thorns.
3. **Twist**: the idea turned (it moves the other way, it
   carries an enemy, it combines with something learned before).
4. **Finish**: a short flourish that runs it at speed, then **the level's
   end**.

The beats are no longer opened by checkpoints: there is one, at the middle
(below), between the test and the twist.

**Inside a beat, a chain** (from the research,
`design/research/platformer-fun.md` §3, Super Mario World's way): a beat is
a run of small challenges on its idea, and **no two in a row are the same**.
Each is the last one made harder (a wider gap, a smaller landing) or the last
one with something added (a height, an enemy, a drop). The chain rises, with
one place to rest in it.

### The land, and things to do in it (the creative lead, after Donkey Kong Country)
"Donkey Kong has a cool style of land, wall terraces and platforms, it also has
secret routes like above on top of trees ... also you can pick things up and
throw them break a wall go into a secret mini game ... everything feels
pretty natural to the environment nothing out of place." Their mockups of
this: `docs/mockups/platformer/natural_traversal/` (a log across a ravine,
a root arch over water, stepped stone terraces, crates on the ledges,
lanterns in tree hollows).

- **Hardly any platforms** (the creative lead: "they hardly ever use
  platforms ... they use a lot of what's in the environment already like
  raising the ground, stacking boxes, or use floating islands"). Height
  comes from the world itself: **raised ground** (banks, mounds, root
  shelves), **wall terraces** (steps of 2 or 3 tiles, in stone or earth),
  **stacked crates** (solid boxes, 2 tiles each, climbed like steps:
  `_box_stack`), **stone pillars and islands** standing in water, and
  **floating islands**. Things you stand on that aren't ground are always
  something real: a fallen log, a root bridge, a treetop, a raft. A floating
  plank is never the answer; a moving one only where something really moves
  (a raft, a lift).
- **Carry and throw:** crates and barrels stand where people left them.
  Kleo lifts one (interact), carries it overhead (a little slower, a little
  lower jump, no ledge catches), and throws it (attack): flat and fast, about
  9 tiles. It fells a weak enemy at once and smashes on what it hits.
- **Cracked walls:** pale, cracked stone that her sword only rings off; a
  thrown crate breaks it open. A tool for a level that wants one, not the
  way into bonus areas (the creative lead: crate throwing "was just an
  example of what Donkey Kong did").
- **Bonus areas** (decided by the creative lead, 2026-11, after DKC 1 and 2:
  "finding something hidden that takes you to another zone even is rad"):
  found by exploring, behind a false wall, at the end of a secret route, in
  a hidden cave. The way in is a hollow glowing faintly with Anima; walking
  in takes her to **another zone** beside the level, out of its view. Two
  kinds: **gather** (beads light up: gather every one before the time runs
  out and the token appears) and **course** (a short run of jumps: reach the
  token at its end in time). The prize is **a bonus token**. Out of time or
  a fall: no harm, back out beside the entrance, try again. **Two per
  level.** From 1-2 on, the creative lead's **Resin rush**: "a ton of resin
  where you need to jump and try and get all the resin; if you're able to
  get it all you get a token": a gather with beads along the arc of every
  jump, whose Resin is hers to keep when she wins.
- **Secret routes high above:** over the treetops, along a ruin's top:
  reached by something a curious player notices (beads climbing a crate
  stack, a raised bank), paid off with Resin, a letter or a bonus entrance.
  In 1-1: a root bank on the hill, a crate, a stack of two, then the
  treetops (letter E, and bonus 2's entrance at their end).
- **Nothing out of place:** everything she stands on is ground, stone, a log or a treetop; every
  crate is where someone would have left it; every secret has a reason to be
  there. In the blockout, backdrop shapes show what holds each piece up.

### The checkpoint: one, at the middle
- **One checkpoint a level, at its middle** (the creative lead, 2026-11:
  "there should be a middle point where you get a checkpoint; I don't want
  lanterns, they look bad"), as Donkey Kong Country's star barrel and
  Mario's midway gate. It is **an Ironbark Guard's banner on a pole**,
  furled until Kleo passes it; then it unfurls and flies. It saves her place
  in the level, refills her flames and banks her Resin. (A stand-in sprite
  until the creative lead's art.)
- It stands **before** the level's hard half, on safe ground.
- Played and confirmed by the creative lead: "1 checkpoint is perfect."
- The level's start is the other place she can come back to.

### Lives or checkpoints: **checkpoints, no lives**
**Proposed:** no lives and no game over. Hurt costs health; **falling in a
pit or water costs one flame and sends her back to the last checkpoint**;
**thorns are a hit** (a flame, and they throw her back out the way she
came: the creative lead "had no idea why I was dying" when thorns sent her
back); losing her last flame sends her back to the last checkpoint with her
health refilled.

Why: lives punish by replaying the parts you already beat, which in a long
handcrafted level means replaying minutes of content you've mastered. Modern
precision platformers (Celeste, Ori, Hollow Knight's benches) all dropped
lives for frequent checkpoints, and the challenge stays in the jump in front
of you. The stake comes from Resin instead: **Resin picked up since the last
checkpoint is dropped when she falls** (the beads go back to where they were,
so nothing is lost for good), which makes a risky secret feel risky without
punishing the player twice.

### Health
- **Lantern flames** (amber, top left; never a red bar). Kleo starts with
  **3**. An enemy hit costs 1, a hazard costs 1 and returns her to the
  checkpoint. After a hit she flickers and can't be hurt for about 1 second.
- **More flames:** up to 8 in all, from the secret levels (one each) and the
  Refuge (bought with Resin).
- **Healing:** a checkpoint refills her; **amber sap** (rare drops, sometimes
  in chests) restores one flame.

### Secrets in every level
Each level hides:
- **The letters K-L-E-O** (the creative lead chose letters over cocoons,
  2026-11: "the cocoons to save people is dumb"), as DKC's K-O-N-G: gold
  letters hung along the way, in order. Two on the main line's jumps, two
  off it (behind a false wall, on a secret route). All four in one level:
  a burst of Resin (25 for now).
- **2 bonus areas**, each won for **a bonus token** (§5, the land).
- **2 to 4 Resin caches** off the path: chests and hidden hollows.
- **One great secret: Morvath's thorn knot** (from the research, Donkey Kong
  Country 2's DK coin): one of her knots, asleep, hidden with more cunning
  than anything else in the level. Touching it wakes it: the amber-gold eye
  opens and stays open. The best hiding places turn the player's own habits
  against them (1-1: behind a bonus entrance that only lets her by once the
  bonus is won; 1-2: back the way she came, the one time the game asks her
  to go left). What the knots do when all are awake is undecided
  (**Proposed:** Morvath's test at the Foundry gate is gentler, or they open
  a last secret).
- **In X-4 or X-5, a hidden exit** to the region's secret level (region 1's
  is in 1-3: the creative lead's map draws the secret's path from pad 3).

Tells, always: motes drifting into a false wall, moss on its stones, a Resin
bead trail leading off the path, a faint Anima glow at a bonus entrance.

**Decided** (the creative lead, 2026-11, "I'll go with suggestions"):
**bonus tokens open each region's secret level** (as DKC2's Kremkoins open
the Lost World), and **each region's boss frees its captives**: beating the
boss sends the people held there to the Refuge, where they open their
services.

### Rules for secrets and Resin (from the research)
- **Resin never lies.** A bead over a drop means safe ground under it. Beads
  in a line show a real jump. A bead out of reach marks a real secret. Never
  a bead over a death.
- **Risky Resin beside the safe line:** a few beads where taking them is a
  small dare (a low stone, a drop and a climb back). The player chooses the
  difficulty one jump at a time.
- **Secrets are meant to be found:** each has a tell, and the tells differ:
  motes into a wall, beads leading off the path, a glow, the secret itself
  in plain sight but out of reach. Not the same one every time (Donkey Kong
  Country 3's mistake).
- **The game keeps score, per level**, on the world map: what is found and
  what is missing.
- **Searching is never punished:** looking round costs time, not flames.

### Resin on the way
Beads lead along the main path in short trails (DKC's bananas): they show
the line of a jump and reward the cautious route. Caches and chests hold the
big amounts. A chest opens with a sword strike and **spills its Resin in an
arc**.

### The level's end
An arched doorway, waystone or gate in the region's style. Walking through
it ends the level: a short tally (Resin, letters of 4, bonus tokens of 2,
secret exit if found), then the world map.

## 6. Resin and what it buys

Resin (the currency; its name and sprite are stand-ins from Lost Keep) is
spent at the Refuge, with the people Kleo rescued:

| Stall (who runs it) | Sells |
|---|---|
| **The healer** | Lantern flames: +1 max health, four for sale at rising prices |
| **The Rootling kitchen** | A meal before a level: one extra flame for that level only |
| **The map-maker** | For each level, how many letters, tokens and secrets are still missing, and a hint for each |
| **The tailor** | Kleo's looks: the wide-brimmed hat and long coat, cloak linings (never red) |
| **The builders** | Homes and gardens for the rescued: the Refuge visibly grows and the Kindreds mix |
| **The archivist** | Concept art, music, the Alchemist's notes (lore) |

Nothing that is needed to finish the game is sold: Resin buys comfort, help
and beauty.

## 7. The hub: the Refuge

- **The world map** of Vaelora is the menu: each region's levels are stops
  along a path. **Built for region 1 on the creative lead's painted map**
  ("Flooded Sanctuary": six numbered pads and a "?" grotto; pads 1 to 5 are
  the five levels, pad 6 at the skull gate is the boss, the grotto is the
  secret level, reached off pad 3). Kleo walks pad to pad along its dotted
  paths; a stop opens when the one it follows is done; a cleared pad is
  ringed in amber. **For the stop she stands on, its record:** the letters K-L-E-O, the
  two bonus tokens and the thorn knot, each shown found or missing: the
  hunt kept in view (from the research: players hunt what they can see is
  missing). The game opens on the map; a level's end leads back to it; Esc
  leaves a level for it.
- **The save file** holds what the map keeps score of and nothing else: each
  level's done mark, letters, tokens and knot, and the Resin. Beads, chests
  and the checkpoint start afresh every time a level is entered.
- The Refuge will sit on the map between regions.
- **The Refuge is a walkable place**, a small handcrafted level with no
  danger, in the forest edge east of the Lost Keep. It starts nearly empty
  with a single campfire; every rescue adds someone, and the stalls open as
  people arrive. Early on the Kindreds keep to their own corners and argue;
  as rescues mount they share fires and songs.
- **After the Reveal** (region 6), the survivors rename it the
  **Memory-Meld Refuge**.
- Morvath, Sora and Vesper appear here between regions; this is where the
  story is told, between the action.
- **The finale:** after the Alchemist, the way opens to the **Sovereign
  Prism**, a last level and the ending. The Lost Keep itself, "the last place
  taken back", is retaken in the epilogue.
